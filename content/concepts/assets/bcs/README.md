# Photographs for the body condition tool

Drop real photographs of horses here and tell Claude. They replace the drawing on the
Learn page.

## What makes a photograph usable

The six Henneke sites have to be visible and markable, which needs:

1. **True side-on.** The horse square to the camera, not angled toward or away. This is the
   one that disqualifies most photographs — including the one of Red we already have, where
   his hindquarters are turned away and his tail covers the dock.
2. **Whole horse in frame**, standing square on level ground, all four legs visible.
3. **Camera at the horse's mid-body height**, not shooting down from above.
4. **Flat, even light.** Overcast is ideal. Hard sun makes shadows that read as ribs.
5. **Plain background** where possible — a barn wall or a solid fence beats a busy paddock.
6. **Short coat.** A winter coat hides everything, which is exactly why January cases get
   missed, and it will hide it from the camera too.

## What we want, in order of usefulness

- One good side-on photograph of a horse at **each** score we can get. Intake photographs
  cover the thin end; sound adopted horses cover the healthy end.
- Failing that, **one** good side-on photograph. Six points can be marked accurately on it,
  and the drawing carries the score-by-score change.

## Naming

`score-1.jpg`, `score-2.jpg` ... `score-9.jpg`, or `lateral-<horsename>.jpg` for a single
reference animal. Any resolution; they get resized on build.

## Consent

Anything showing a horse from an active or recent case needs to be cleared before it goes on
a page, even a private one. Check with Dan if a photograph came from a law-enforcement seizure.
